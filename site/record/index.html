PRO · CFB desk

Trench leans, against the week's median

A target (Rush Edge or Stacked Rush) hits when the flagged team's yards per carry beat that week's FBS median; a fade hits when they fall below it. Because the bar is the median, a coin flip wins half — anything above that is the trench edge showing up.

PRO · NFL desk

Spots and props, against the closing number

Game spots are graded against the archived closing total, cold team-total leans against the implied team total, and priced props against the line we archived. A push isn't graded, and WATCH spots — games flagged to watch, not to play — aren't graded at all.

PRO · PGA desk

Course horses, against a top-20 bar

A Course Horse target hits on a top-20 finish; a golfer who never tees off is left ungraded. A top 20 is easier in a small field, so each event shows its field size and the base rate — the share of that field a random pick would have hit.

PRO · NASCAR desk

Track targets, against a top-10 bar

A Track Target hits on a top-10 finish; a Dominator — the stronger claim — needs a top 5; a fade hits when the driver finishes outside the bar. A driver who doesn't start is left ungraded. The base rate is what a random driver would have hit against the same bars.

Free · MLB radar

The free radar's own calls

The free MLB radar makes three kinds of call, each graded the next morning. An O/U lean when similar-weather games at the park landed a full run off the day's total. An HR call when those games moved home runs 15% or more against the park's average, graded against that average. A K call the same way on strikeouts, at 6%. Early cards used an older, looser lean rule; they're graded as they posted.
