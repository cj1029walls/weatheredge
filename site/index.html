What it actually does

Not a weather app. A venue-history engine.

Anyone can tell you it'll be 38° and windy. The useful question is what 38° and windy has historically done to scoring at that specific stadium — and whether the number on the board already accounts for it.

🌡️

Forecast at the venue

Hour-by-hour temperature, wind, rain and humidity pulled for each venue's own coordinates and read at the exact kickoff or first-pitch hour — in local time, not a city-level average.

📚

That venue's own history

Every past game at the same venue in similar conditions, going back years. College football alone carries 6,795 Power 4 games from 2010 onward, era-adjusted so an older scoring environment doesn't read as a weather effect.

🎯

Against the real closing number

Over/under rates are measured against the closing total that was actually on the board that day — not a reconstructed line. If the sample is too thin to be trusted, the page says so instead of showing a percentage.

🏟️

See the conditions

A live 3D view of each park or stadium, oriented and lit to the forecast, with wind streamlines showing which way the ball is actually going to move.

How it works

Four steps, rebuilt every morning

The whole board regenerates on a schedule as forecasts sharpen toward kickoff, so what you see in the morning is not what you saw two days ago.

Read the slate

Pull every game inside the forecast window, with its venue, start time and the current market total.

Match the conditions

Find past games at that venue inside a stated temperature and wind window, and report how many matched.

Measure the impact

Compare scoring and the underlying box-score signals against both the league baseline and the venue's own average.

Grade it in public

Every PRO call settles from the final box score and goes into a running record — losing stretches included.
