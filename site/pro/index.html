How the number is made

When lineups post (~4 PM ET), plate appearances follow the batting slot (leadoff ≈ 4.65, nine-hole ≈ 3.6) and benched players drop off the card; before lineups, everyone gets a flat 4.0. Each batter's three-season HR rate is shrunk toward the league average (small samples can't fake an edge), then adjusted by tonight's weather at the park (DFSRADAR's similar-conditions history), the plate umpire's HR tendency (shrunk hard, capped at ±10% — tightened after our public reliability tracking showed ump tendencies holding in only ~half of non-neutral games), and the opposing starter's HR/9 (shrunk by innings). The whole card also tracks a trailing two-week league HR environment from our own graded results, so a league-wide hot or cold stretch moves every number with it. That rate over ~4 plate appearances gives the HR chance. Expected HRs per game is the sum of those individual probabilities. Edge compares our chance to the books' median price with ~13 points of vig stripped out (one-sided HR markets run heavy); when a live price exists, 40% of the market's implied probability is blended into ours. Career batter-vs-pitcher history is shown for context only — our graded receipts showed it adds noise, not signal (flagged targets hit 1 of 15), so it no longer moves a probability. A VALUE flag needs a 5-point edge, at least 3 books, and +150 or longer — at most five per night. With-ump splits (how tonight's batters and starters have fared with this exact plate umpire, 2022–2026) are context only — those samples are far too small to move a probability, and we won't pretend otherwise. Game totals blend season offense, the opposing starter's shrunk ERA, the park's weather runs history, and the ump's runs tendency. K projections = shrunk K/9 × shrunk innings per start × the weather strikeout edge at half weight × the plate ump's strikeout environment (shrunk, capped ±6% — a wide zone is worth real Ks), shown against the line with no OVER/UNDER call — we retired K lean calls on 8/25 after a 57-call public test ran 47%, and the full record stays on the Record tab. The Record tab also grades our per-game Expected HRs (average error, share within ±1 and ±2) and tracks whether non-neutral umpires' tendencies actually held — if that reliability number sags, we shrink the ump factor harder. The receipts tune the model. Live scores and HR tracking come from MLB's free public stats API and cost nothing. Every night's card is archived and graded — the calibration table is the receipts.
